Unit 1 • 2 Marks

Unit 1: Communication Skills-II

Session 1 – Methods of Communication

Communication comes from Latin communicare = “to share”. It is exchange of information, ideas, feelings between sender and receiver through speech, writing, signals or behaviour.

MethodFormsExamplesStrength / Limit
Verbal – OralFace-to-face, phone, meeting, speech, classroom Q&ATeacher explaining DBMS; viva answerFast + feedback; no record, forgotten
Verbal – WrittenLetters, circulars, notes, reports, email, SMSLeave application; email to principalRecord + reference; slow, no tone
Non-verbalBody language, gestures, posture, facial expression, eye contact, tone, touch, spaceSmiling + nodding in interview; crossed arms = closedShows true feeling; can be misread
VisualCharts, graphs, signs, diagrams, photos, videos, iconsTraffic signs; pie-chart in spreadsheet; flowchartQuick for illiterate/large data; needs design
Exam line: “Verbal uses words (oral/written); non-verbal uses body without words; visual uses images/graphs.” Give 2 examples each for full marks.

Session 2 – Communication Cycle & Feedback

Cycle: Sender → Message → Encoding → Channel → Receiver → Decoding → Feedback

  • Sender: initiates idea. Message: content. Encoding: converting idea to words/symbols/diagram. Channel: medium (face-to-face, phone, email, TV). Receiver: target. Decoding: interpreting. Feedback: response back – completes loop.
  • Example: Teacher (sender) explains SQL (message) in English + board (encoding + channel) → students (receivers) interpret (decoding) → ask doubts / nod (feedback).

Feedback – why crucial? Confirms understanding, corrects errors, motivates, builds trust. No feedback = one-way, incomplete communication.

TypeMeaningExample
DescriptiveDetailed guidance on what was good + what to improve“Your query is correct, but add WHERE clause to filter Class 10.”
SpecificPinpoints exact item/person/point“Slide 3 font is too small.”
Non-specific / GeneralVague praise or criticism, no detail“Good job!” / “Not good.”
Positive / Negative / ConstructiveAppreciate / point fault / fault + solutionConstructive: “Spelling wrong in para 2 – check dictionary and rewrite.”

Session 3 – Barriers to Communication

A barrier is any hindrance in sending, receiving or understanding a message.

BarrierMeaningExample + Fix
PhysicalNoise, distance, bad network, closed doorsTraffic noise in online class → mute, use headphones, sit closer
Linguistic / LanguageUnknown language, jargon, difficult wordsTeacher uses “normalisation” without explaining → use simple words + glossary
CulturalDifferent customs, festivals, gesturesThumbs-up rude in some cultures → learn audience culture
Emotional / PsychologicalFear, anger, mistrust, sadness, stage fearStudent afraid to ask doubt → create friendly environment
InterpersonalEgo, prejudice, poor self-esteem, generation gapSenior ignores junior’s idea → listen actively, respect
Organisational / PerceptualLong hierarchy, wrong assumption, past experience biasMessage filtered through 3 monitors → keep chain short, confirm in writing

Session 4 – The 7 Cs of Effective Communication

CMeansExample
ClearEasy words, one idea at a time“Submit practical file by Monday 9 am” vs “Submit soon”
ConciseBrief, no extra words“Meeting at 10 am, Room 4”
ConcreteSpecific + facts/figures“Scores rose from 72% to 88%”
CorrectTrue facts + correct grammar/spellingCheck dates, names, spell-check email
CoherentLogical flow, connected pointsIntro → steps → conclusion
CompleteAll needed info (who/what/when/where)Notice has date, venue, contact
CourteousPolite, respectful, no slang“Please”, “Thank you”, “Sorry for delay”

Session 5 – Writing Skills (must for 2 marks)

  • Parts of sentence: Subject + Verb + Object. Ex: Students (S) write (V) notes (O).
  • Punctuation: Capital letter to start + full stop for statement, ? for question, ! for order/excitement, comma for pause/list, apostrophe for possession (Ravi’s book).
  • Common errors: SMS slang in formal letter, long paragraph without break, no date/venue in notice.
  • Formats to practise: Notice, email, paragraph (100-120 words), report – all need heading, date, clear points.
Perspective: Everyone filters messages through visual perception, language, past experience, prejudice, environment, beliefs, feelings (hostile/joyful/sad). Good communicators keep audience perspective in mind.
Unit 2 • 2 Marks

Unit 2: Self-Management Skills-II

Session 1 – Stress, Stressors, ABC Model

Stress = mental/emotional strain from adverse or demanding situations (exam pressure, deadlines). Stressor = factor causing stress: exams, homework pile, family expectations, illness, noisy home, poor internet, comparison with others.

ABC of Stress Management (Albert Ellis):

A – AdversityB – BeliefsC – Consequences
The event itself (Maths paper is tough)How you think about it (“I will fail” vs “I can revise 3 chapters”)Emotion + action (panic & blank vs calm plan & attempt)

Change B to change C – same paper, positive self-talk gives better result. This is the most-asked 2-mark Q.

Session 2 – Stress-Management Techniques (learn 4–5 with example)

CategoryTechniquesHow to do (10 min)
BodyExercise, yoga, deep breathing, stretchingWalk 20 min / Anulom-vilom 5 min / 4-7-8 breathing
MindMeditation, positive self-talk, journalingWrite 3 worries + 1 solution each; say “I am prepared”
Joy / NatureMusic, hobby, time in nature, family talkGardening, drawing, playing, talking to friend – no phone 30 min
PlanningTo-do list, Eisenhower quadrants, SMART goals, breaks (Pomodoro 25-5)List 6 tasks → Do Important-Urgent first; sleep 7–8 hrs
Healthy habits: 7–8 hrs sleep, less junk food, less screen before bed, ask teacher/counsellor for help – seeking help is strength, not weakness.

Session 3 – Ability to Work Independently (3 pillars)

PillarMeaningStudent example
Self-awarenessKnowing strengths, weaknesses, values, triggers“I am good at theory, weak at SQL JOIN – so I give 40 min extra to JOIN.” SWOT of self.
Self-motivationInner drive without external push; goals, optimismRevise daily without parents reminding; reward after 2 hrs study.
Self-regulationControlling emotions, habits, time; disciplinePhone on silent during study; control anger in group project; follow timetable.

SMART goals (use in answer): Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic/Relevant, Time-bound. Ex: “Score 8/10 in Part A by revising 1 unit daily till Friday” is SMART; “Study hard” is not.

Time-management quadrants: I Urgent+Important (exam tomorrow – DO), II Important+Not urgent (daily revision – PLAN), III Urgent+Not important (random calls – DELEGATE/LIMIT), IV Neither (endless reels – AVOID).

Unit 3 • 2 Marks

Unit 3: ICT Skills-II

Session 1 – Operating System (OS)

OS = system software that manages hardware (CPU, memory, disk), files, users and runs all programs. Acts as bridge between user and hardware. Ex: Windows 10/11, Ubuntu Linux, macOS, Android.

Functions: process & memory management, file management, device/I-O management, user management & security, error handling.

TypeMeaningExample
Single-user / Single-taskOne user, one taskMS-DOS
Single-user / Multi-task (Interactive/GUI)One user, many apps with mouse/windowsWindows, Ubuntu Desktop
Multi-userMany users together via accounts/networkLinux server, Unix
Real-Time (RTOS)Instant response in millisecondsAirbag, ATM, washing-machine controller
Distributed / Mobile / NetworkRuns across many machines / phonesAndroid, Google servers

Session 2 – Files, Folders & Extensions

File = collection of data with name + extension. Folder/Directory = container. Path = address. Ex: C:\Class10\IT\practical.ods or /home/student/notes.pdf

ExtensionTypeOpens in
.txt, .docx/.odt, .pdfText / DocumentNotepad, Writer/Word, Reader
.ods/.xlsx, .odp/.pptxSpreadsheet / PresentationCalc/Excel, Impress/PowerPoint
.jpg/.png, .mp3/.mp4Image / Audio-VideoGallery, VLC
.zip, .exe, .htmlCompressed / App / WebExtractor, installer, browser

Operations: Create → Rename (F2/right-click) → Copy (Ctrl+C) → Paste (Ctrl+V) → Cut/Move (Ctrl+X) → Delete (Del → Recycle Bin/Trash) → Restore / Permanent delete (Shift+Del). Naming: no \ / : * ? " < > |, keep short, use dates: IT_U3_Notes_2026-10-07.pdf.

Session 3 – Computer Care & Maintenance

AreaDoDon’t
HardwareMicrofibre cloth for screen, air vents clear, UPS/stabiliser, keyboard coverEat/drink near PC, block fan, pull plug without shutdown
SoftwareUpdate OS/apps, antivirus scan weekly, uninstall unused apps, strong passwordsInstall from unknown links, click pop-up “You won”, share OTP
Disk toolsDisk Cleanup: deletes temp files. Defragment: rearranges HDD pieces for speed (not needed for SSD). Backup: copy to external/cloud.Delete system files, full disk 100%

Malware: Virus (needs host + human click, ex: infected pen-drive), Worm (self-spreads via network), Trojan (fake useful app), Spyware/Ransomware (steals/locks data for money). Safety: antivirus + firewall ON, don’t open unknown email attachments, scan USB, backup with 3-2-1 rule (3 copies, 2 media, 1 offsite/cloud).

2-mark ready lines: “Disk Cleanup removes temporary/junk files to free space; Defragmentation rearranges scattered HDD data for faster access.” + “Firewall blocks unauthorised network access.”
Unit 4 • 2 Marks

Unit 4: Entrepreneurial Skills-II

TermMeaningExample
EntrepreneurPerson who spots a problem/need, creates innovative solution, organises resources, takes calculated risk, starts enterpriseGirl making eco-friendly paper bags for local shops
EntrepreneurshipProcess/mindset of starting + running venturePlanning, funding, marketing, handling loss
EnterpriseThe business unit itselfBakery, mobile-repair shop, ed-tech startup

Characteristics (learn any 6 – most asked)

  1. Passionate & self-confident – believes in idea despite failures.
  2. Hard-working & disciplined – long hours, follows plan.
  3. Creative & innovative – new product or better process (ex: tiffin subscription).
  4. Risk-taker (calculated) – researches then invests, keeps backup.
  5. Adaptable & open to feedback – changes menu/price as per customers.
  6. Decision-maker & leader – hires, guides team.
  7. Continuous learner, ethical – learns GST, digital marketing; honest quality.

Functions & Role in Society

  • Functions: idea generation → feasibility check → organising money/men/material → managing production/marketing → bearing risk & profit.
  • Role: creates employment, boosts GDP/taxes, uses local resources, brings innovation, improves living standard, reduces migration (village jobs).

Myths vs Realities (table = full 2 marks)

MythReality
Entrepreneurs are born, not madeSkills (planning, selling, accounting) can be learned & nurtured in school/EAP
Need huge capital to startMany start small/home-based with minimal funds (cloud kitchen, tuition, repair)
Must invent something brand-newImproving existing service (faster delivery, cheaper) also succeeds
Only for business families / high marksAnyone with idea + discipline; failures are stepping stones

Attitudes/values for success: problem-solving attitude, customer first, quality, respect for time/money, social responsibility (eco-friendly, fair wages).

Unit 5 • 2 Marks

Unit 5: Green Skills-II

Session 1 – Sustainable Development

Definition (Brundtland, most asked): “Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.”

3 Pillars: Environment (protect nature) + Society (health, equity, education) + Economy (jobs, growth without waste). If any pillar breaks, development is unsustainable.

Problems of unsustainable development: air/water pollution, deforestation, e-waste, water scarcity, climate change, poverty.

Session 2 – 17 SDGs (UN, 2015 → target 2030)

Aim: end poverty, protect planet, peace & prosperity. Learn at least 6 with numbers for exam:

2Zero HungerMid-day meals, no food waste
3Good Health & Well-beingVaccination, sports
4Quality EducationFree digital notes (like this site)
5Gender EqualityEqual lab chances for girls
6Clean Water & SanitationRainwater harvesting in school
7Affordable & Clean EnergySolar panels on roof
11Sustainable CitiesPublic transport, segregation
12Responsible Consumption & Production4Rs, no single-use plastic
13Climate ActionPlant trees, save electricity
14–15Life Below Water / Life on LandNo plastic in rivers; protect forests

Full list for reference: 1 No Poverty, 2 Zero Hunger, 3 Health, 4 Education, 5 Gender, 6 Water, 7 Energy, 8 Decent Work, 9 Industry & Innovation, 10 Reduced Inequalities, 11 Cities, 12 Consumption, 13 Climate, 14 Water-life, 15 Land-life, 16 Peace & Justice, 17 Partnerships.

Session 3 – Green Economy, Green Jobs, 4Rs

Green Economy: economy that improves well-being + social equity while reducing environmental risks and saving resources. Sectors: renewable energy, green buildings, clean transport, water/waste management, sustainable farming.

Green Jobs: decent jobs that preserve/restore environment. Ex: solar installer, wind technician, e-waste recycler, organic farmer, EV mechanic, water auditor, green architect.

RMeansExample
ReduceUse lessBoth-side printing, cloth bag
ReuseUse againJam bottles as pen stands, old uniforms as dusters
RecycleConvert waste to newPaper → notebooks; plastic → benches
RecoverExtract energy/material from wasteBiogas from kitchen waste, compost
+ Refuse/Rethink (extra)Say no to harmfulRefuse single-use straws

Individual actions (write any 4 in exam): switch off lights/fans, LED bulbs, public transport/cycle, segregate wet/dry waste, carry steel bottle, plant trees, repair before replace, e-bills not paper.

Green project idea (practical file): “Solar study lamp + waste segregation in my home for 15 days – saved X units, composted Y kg.” Photos + bill before/after = full practical marks.

High-Yield 2-Mark & 4-Mark Q-Bank (with model answers)

  1. Explain any two types of feedback with examples. [U1]
    → Specific feedback pinpoints exact point (“Table heading missing in Q2”) while descriptive gives detailed guidance (“Query correct but add ORDER BY; check NCERT p.42”). Both close the communication loop.
  2. List any four barriers and suggest one remedy each. [U1]
    → Physical–sit closer/use mic; Language–simple words; Emotional–friendly tone; Cultural–know customs. (Table above.)
  3. What are the 7Cs? Explain any four. [U1]
    → Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, Courteous – with one-line example each.
  4. Explain ABC of stress with an exam example. [U2]
    → A=Tough paper, B=“I can attempt stepwise” vs “I will fail”, C=calm attempt vs panic. Change belief → better consequence.
  5. Suggest four stress-management techniques for board exams. [U2]
    → 30-min walk/yoga, 4-7-8 breathing, Pomodoro timetable + 8-hr sleep, talk to teacher/friend + hobby break.
  6. Differentiate self-awareness, self-motivation, self-regulation. [U2]
    → Awareness=know strengths; Motivation=inner drive; Regulation=control habits/emotions – one example each.
  7. What is an OS? Explain any two types with examples. [U3]
    → Bridge software managing hardware/files. Single-user GUI (Windows), Multi-user (Linux server), RTOS (airbag/ATM).
  8. What is file management? Explain extensions with 4 examples. [U3]
    → Organising files/folders/paths. .docx=document, .ods=sheet, .jpg=image, .pdf=portable – plus copy/move/rename/delete steps.
  9. Explain virus, worm, Trojan and two preventive measures. [U3]
    → Virus needs host+click, worm self-spreads, Trojan fake app. Update antivirus, firewall ON, don’t open unknown attachments, scan USB, 3-2-1 backup.
  10. Who is an entrepreneur? Give four characteristics. [U4]
    → Spots need + innovates + risks + enterprise. Passionate, hard-working, creative, calculated risk-taker, adaptable, leader.
  11. Bust two myths of entrepreneurship. [U4]
    → Born-not-made → skills can be learnt; huge capital needed → many start small/home-based.
  12. Define sustainable development and its three pillars. [U5]
    → Brundtland definition + Environment/Society/Economy with one threat each.
  13. Write any four SDGs with one action each. [U5]
    → Ex: 4 Quality Education–free notes; 6 Clean Water–harvesting; 7 Clean Energy–solar; 13 Climate–plant trees.
  14. What are green jobs? Give four examples + explain 4Rs. [U5]
    → Jobs saving environment (solar installer, e-waste recycler, organic farmer, EV mechanic) + Reduce/Reuse/Recycle/Recover table.
